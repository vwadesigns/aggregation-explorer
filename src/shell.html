A process, not a crossfade.

Each seed defines the starting continuous fields. The density-dependent aggregation (DDA) equations exchange material between a slower retained field u and a faster mobile field v.

R = u·v − a·u / (b + u)
u_next = u + dt · ( R + Da · Laplacian(u))
v_next = v + dt · (−R + Db · Laplacian(v))

The calculation uses separate old/new states. Nothing is repeatedly blurred and thresholded into the next simulation step. Thresholding is display/export only.

What is adapted: seeded irregular starting patches, fixed starting material budget, synchronous numerical updates, cell-face boundaries, checkpoint replay, and the browser interface. The reference starting mode uses small paired perturbations rather than the notebook’s two independent noise fields.

What is not claimed: this is not the exact Texel dune-grass animation, a wind/sand-height simulation, a WM plug-in, or a scientifically calibrated terrain model. Coarsening can be slow; larger model age does not guarantee fewer components at every stage. Finite grids can affect late-time behavior.

Moving backward restores continuous-field checkpoints and replays forward. Evicted cache entries are rebuilt from an earlier state. A setup saves the recipe and selected age, not the whole history. Results are repeatable within a fixed version, grid, and backend; CPU/GPU pixel identity is not guaranteed.

Source: Siteur et al. (2023), Phase-separation physics underlies new theory for the resilience of patchy ecosystems, PNAS 120(2), e2202683120. Paper · Research repository.
